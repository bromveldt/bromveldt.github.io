# Ireland IT contract market — channels for a remote, NL-based freelancer

Finding Irish IT contract projects (Java / Spring Boot / Kubernetes, AI-assisted dev) delivered remotely through Osterholm B.V.

**Why Ireland fits you:** it works in English (no language gate), Dublin is the European base for the US multinationals (your US-rigor + native-English + EU bundle reads as an asset there), and Ireland has a mature day-rate contracting culture with none of Germany's AnÜ anxiety. Big Tech in Ireland is going contractor-first for 2026 AI and regulatory/compliance projects — your lane.

**Residency / company — you don't need Irish residency for remote B2B.** Osterholm B.V. invoices the Irish client and the client reverse-charges Irish VAT (net invoice, same mechanics as Germany). An Irish Ltd, umbrella, or Irish tax residency is only needed for on-site / PAYE / umbrella contract structures — screen each role for whether they'll engage a non-resident company remotely.

**Geography:** Dublin ≈ 45% of roles and the home of remote-friendly software/AI work; Cork = cybersecurity. Galway (MedTech) and Limerick (hardware) skew on-site — weak for remote B2B software.

**Reachability — publish a real, dialable mobile, plus email and LinkedIn.** Irish IT recruiters routinely contact international candidates and run heavily on email and LinkedIn, so a real mobile (your existing +49 or +31) together with your LinkedIn is enough for this market — unlike German, Austrian or Italian agents, Irish recruiters won't balk at dialling a foreign number. Do **not** rely on WhatsApp: many European recruiters need a normal, traceable, billable phone call and won't use it (the DACH and Italian markets especially). A local +353 line is a nice signal but unnecessary and not easily obtained — Irish numbers carry a ComReg local-address requirement that every tier enforces (on Zadarma even the national 0818 and toll-free 1800 demand an address in Ireland), so as a non-resident you hit a wall. Treat that as confirmation of the pattern, not a to-do.

## B. Where to watch listings

- **LinkedIn** (filter: Ireland · Contract · Remote) — but treat open ads as the low-yield channel; agencies hold the unadvertised roles.
- **IrishJobs.ie** and agency newsletters/job alerts.

## C. Direct targets worth approaching

- **US multinationals in Dublin** (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Salesforce, Workday, etc.) — contractor-first for AI/compliance; usually engaged via their preferred agencies/MSPs rather than direct, but the work matches you.
- **Irish software firms & consultancies that use contractors** — e.g. Version 1, Storm Technology, Auxilion, Zartis (remote dev teams) — more likely to contract a non-resident remotely than a big-tech MSP.

## How to work the list

1. Register with 5–6 agencies above; once in their database, contract roles come to you. Lead with the English CV and the AI/migration positioning.
2. For each role, confirm two things early: remote acceptable, and they'll contract a non-resident company (Osterholm B.V.) rather than requiring an Irish Ltd/umbrella.
3. Invoice net with the reverse-charge note; the Irish client self-accounts Irish VAT.
4. Keep the tax checklist in mind — billing Irish clients is clean, but *your* residency/PE position still follows where you physically are, not where the client is. Settle that with your adviser before signing.
