## The throughline: this hunt *is* your permit strategy

Income, your return, and the 2030 permit are one system, not three errands. A Dutch contract — especially a remote one you start from Cologne — does three jobs at once:

- **Funds the return** (and any double-rent overlap with the Cologne lease ending end of October).
- **Keeps the BV trading**, which the IND requires for the self-employed permit (and which counts toward any future permanent-residence file).
- **Evidences that your centre of interests is still Dutch** — a live NL client invoiced through your BV is exactly the behaviour that supports a "temporary absence, returning" reading if the IND ever asks.

So treat every step below as load-bearing twice over.

## Market reality (2026) — read before you apply

- **Wet DBA is being enforced.** The handhavingsmoratorium ended 1 Jan 2025; 2026 is a "soft landing" (company visits and corrections first, no verzuimboetes), but the Belastingdienst can levy back-payroll-tax to Jan 2025 and *vergrijpboetes* for intent. Clients know this and have grown cautious about direct freelance engagements.
- **Brokers now de-risk the deal.** Because the DBA liability lands on the *client*, many route contractors through a detacheerder/broker who carries the risk. That makes brokers your fastest yes right now — register with several even if direct work pays a little more.
- **A BV helps but doesn't immunise.** Schijnzelfstandigheid is judged on the actual relationship (authority, embedding, personal labour), not the legal vehicle. Win the argument with: deliverable-scoped work, multiple clients, your own tools/risk, and a Belastingdienst *modelovereenkomst*.
- **Channel sets your rate ceiling.** Local channels (Striive, brokers, direct) sustain ~€75–95/hr for a senior. Global marketplaces (Upwork, Toptal, Arc, lemon.io) compress the same profile to ~€45–60/hr. Lead local; treat global as fallback.

## Rate strategy

**Your history:** €65/hr previously. **Your target:** €70+ — which is conservative; treat it as a floor, not the aim.

- **€70 is the bottom of the senior band.** Local senior contract rates run ~€70–90/hr; €75–85 is the realistic centre for a strong 8-year profile, and €90+ needs a scarcity story (rare stack, architecture, hard deadline).
- **Anchor above your floor.** If €72–75 is the least you'll accept, open at **€82–85** and let negotiation settle in the high 70s. Opening at "€70+" lands you at €65–68 — your old rate, minus three years of inflation.
- **Don't disclose the €65.** Whoever names the first number sets the gravity; make it you, and make it high. Quote forward on value, not history.
- **The number compounds.** €78 vs €70 over a year of billing (~1,600 hrs) is ~€13k. Income pressure is real, but caving €8/hr to close a week sooner is a bad trade across a 6–12 month contract.
- **Channel moves the rate.** Brokers add margin *on top* of your quote — give them your net rate and let them mark up to the client. Direct clients pay more (no middle layer). Avoid global marketplaces — they cap a senior at ~€45–60 regardless of seniority.
- German is a quiet premium for Amsterdam firms serving the DACH market — worth a line when relevant.

### Rate line — scripts

Quote everything **ex VAT (BTW 21%)** and say so.

**Anchor (broker asks your rate):**
> "€85 per hour, ex VAT, invoiced through my own BV on a DBA-compliant model agreement. There's some room depending on scope, duration, and onsite expectations."

**Spoken, on a call:**
> "I work at €85 an hour, ex VAT, through my BV. For a longer or clearly scoped engagement I've got a little room, but that's my starting point."

**Direct client (anchor higher — no broker margin):**
> "€90 an hour, ex VAT, via my BV, DBA-compliant. Flexible on scope and duration for the right project."

**Pushback — three moves, in order:**
1. *Trade, don't drop:* "I can look at the high 70s for a six-month-plus commitment, or if it's fully remote with autonomy over delivery. Where's the budget sitting?"
2. *Flush their number:* "What rate has the client set for the role?"
3. *Hold the floor:* "€75 is about as far as I'd go, and that's for the right project. Below that doesn't work for a senior contract through a BV."

**Keep out of the 'cheap remote' bucket (say it early while remote from Cologne):**
> "I'm Netherlands-based, currently remote and relocating to the Randstad this autumn — so NL rates, NL contract through my BV."
