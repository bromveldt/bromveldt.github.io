In 2013 in Munich I was filling out a hospital form about whom to contact in the event I did not wake up after an operation on my broken wrist. The eventuality of passing away under anesthesia made realize that I could possible not look forward to a future. Then it occurred to me then that I did not know anything about my ancestry either. My parents did not tell any stories. It was rumored that some relatives had compiled some lists but I did not have a ready access to those and generally cannot imagine see why a collection of dead people's names should catch my fancy. Except for an approximate life period, nothing was known about them, often not where they lived. My maternal grandfather was a Siberian *starozhil* whose family had stayed put in the same God-forsaken village for centuries. It was not exciting for me.

After I was released from the hospital I ordered a genealogy kit from FamilyTreeDNA. And then another one. And then another one... I ended up with the full mtDNA and the Y-700 test results, a family tree at MyHeritage and uploaded autosomal results on YFull.com.

Thus, highly likely belonging to the genetic group “Russia”. Well, that much is definitely true.

While on the autosomal level I seem to most resemble people from the area south of Penza, Russia where my father was born, my mtDNA results are a bit of an outlier and rather point to Northern Europe. My mother's family was from Siberia, her mother's family, if I recall it correctly, migrated to Siberia to build the Siberian railroad in 1890's, an least a decade before the agrarian reforms implemented by Stolypin. Her father's family from a village in Oryol gouvernment, whereas her mother's familly came from Kostroma government. Kostroma is indeed in the North but lies quite at a distance from Great Novgorod and more close to the Urals and Siberia than to anything Nordic.

## mtDNA matches on my control region

I have 4074 matches on HVR1 and just 22 on HVR2 + HVR1 and 3 (2 full and 1 with 3 different mutations). It would mean that 20 other people (the one imperfect match is not listed by FamilyTreeDNA among my 22 control region matches) have the most recent common ancestor with me around 5450 BCE. Hardly a genealogical breakthrough.

Wikipedia [writes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic): places the beginning of the Neolitic around 650

> Most of Western Europe followed over the next two thousand years, but in some parts of Northwest Europe the neolithic era began much later and lasted just under 3,000 years from c. 4500 BC–1700 BC.
> Through a combination of cultural diffusion and migration of peoples, the Neolithic traditions spread west and northwards to reach northwestern Europe by around 4500 BC.
> With some exceptions, population levels rose rapidly at the beginning of the Neolithic until they reached the carrying capacity.[58] This was followed by a population crash of "enormous magnitude" after 5000 BC, with levels remaining low during the next 1,500 years.[58]

The article on [Neolithic decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_decline):
> The population increase between 5950 and 5550 BP (4000 to 3600 BC) that preceded the decline was catalysed by the introduction of agriculture, along with the spread of technologies such as pottery, the wheel, and animal husbandry. After the Neolithic decline, there were massive human migrations from the Pontic–Caspian steppe into eastern and central Europe, beginning approximately 4800 BP (2850 BC).

Hence all those matches are from the times before the rapid Neolithic expansion.
