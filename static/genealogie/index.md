In 2013 in Munich I was filling out a hospital form about whom to contact in the event I did not wake up after an operation on my broken wrist. The eventuality of passing away under anesthesia made realize that I could possible not look forward to a future. Then it occurred to me then that I did not know anything about my ancestry either. My parents did not tell any stories. It was rumored that some relatives had compiled some lists but I did not have a ready access to those and generally cannot imagine see why a collection of dead people's names should catch my fancy. Except for an approximate life period, nothing was known about them, often not where they lived. My maternal grandfather was a Siberian *starozhil* whose family had stayed put in the same God-forsaken village for centuries. It was not exciting for me.

After I was released from the hospital I ordered a genealogy kit from FamilyTreeDNA. And then another one. And then another one... I ended up with the full mtDNA and the Y-700 test results, a family tree at MyHeritage and uploaded autosomal results to YFull.com (Kit Number YF134794) and to [GED Match](https://app.gedmatch.com/) (Kit Number JX6924082 | GEDCOM Number 8229563).

I clearly seem to have something indistinctly Siberian or Asian in the woodpile. East Asian appears to be somewhat exaggerated. West Siberia should be more like it.

Family Tree DNA's myOrigins&reg; v3 makes me a poster boy for East Slavic heritage: a typical product of the Asian conquest, northbound migration out of the Kievan Rus, Ivan Grozny's Oprischchina and the later Muscovite Troubles, Peter the Great, the abolition of peasant bondage and the Bolshevik Revolution, the Second World War, Stalinism, the post-Khrushchev liberalization of public life in the 1960's and the Perestroika. The Perestroika is incidentally what I last saw of Russia.

Being 5% non-European seems to be a characteristic of an East Slavic descent.

While on the autosomal level I seem to most resemble people from the area south of Penza, Russia where my father was born, my mtDNA results are a bit of an outlier and rather point to Northern Europe. My mother's family was from Siberia, her mother's family, if I recall it correctly, migrated to Siberia to build the Great Siberian railroad in the 1890's, tn least a decade before the agrarian reforms implemented by Stolypin. Her father's family from a village in Oryol government, whereas her mother's family came from Kostroma government. Kostroma is indeed in the North but lies quite at a distance from Great Novgorod and closer to the Urals and Siberia than to anything Nordic.
