The fun fact is that according to FamilyTreeDNA I (may) share a common paternal line ancestor with Rev. **Martin Luther** (1483-1546 CE). That shared ancestor lived around 450 BCE. Compared to the 20+ thousand years TMRCA estimate I get on other famous people's DNA, this is remarkably recent.

The latest defining mutation, the philogenetic parent Luther and I have in common is **S20602**, deemed coincident with I-Y3120 in the literature.

I-Y3120 is considered a marker for distinctively Eastern European ancestry.

Luther inherited the mutation S20602 → FT80992 that had occurred before 1 BCE.

I inherited my haplogroup's defining mutation Y3120 → A16681 that had occurred around 100 CE.

How it is possible that the divergence took place at the dawn of the common era, I am not to know.
The Wikipedia puts I-CTS10228 at 5,100-3,400 YBP and I-Y3120 at 3,400-2,100 YBP, while [Genetic Homeland](https://www.genetichomeland.com/dna-marker/chromosome-Y/S20602) estimates S20602, that is believed to coincide with Y3120 / FGC12085  at 6,202 YBP. I am not knowledgeable enough to assess if those estimates be down to an outdated state of research or to some discrepancies between the ISOGG, YFull and FTDNA phylogenetic trees respectively.

My immediate Y-DNA matches on [YFull.com](http://yfull.com/) are from Poland (from the Lublin and Malopolska regions, both at the eastern edge of contemporary Poland), and one medieval DNA match from Sunghir near Vladimir, Russia. This distribution points east and also rather Northern geographic belt, rather the fabled Pontic steppe.

Let's trust FTDNA and assume that the MRCA lived around 450 BCE, which the midpoint of Iron Age in Central Europe. The early Iron Age, known as the ‘West-Hallstattkreis’ culture, lasted from 800 to 475 BCE, the late Iron Age, known as the La Tène culture from in 450 BCE to 15 BCE in France, Germany and Switzerland. There is of course no indication that the MRCA lived in Central and Western Europe.

### Martin Luther's Family Background

Martin Luther was born in 1483 in Eisleben, County Mansfeld in the Holy Roman Empire (Grafschaft Mansfeld, Sachsen, Heiliges Römisches Reich) but before his birth his parents lived for a number of years in Möhra in County Thüringen, to the south-west of Mansfeld. His father's family called Möhra home for generations and was trying to set aside of its hereditary nobility &ndash; and thus avoid having to serve in the military &ndash; for a more rewarding entrepreneurial pursuit (Martin Luther's father was a leaseholder of copper mines and smelters in the mining area Mansfeld-Südharz).

It is worthwhile to remember that from 1300 Europe was entering the Little Glacial Age. The temperatures were falling, rains were pouring, breaking flat roofs and giving rise to the Gothic style in architecture. After a couple of centuries of that, Constantinople would fall to Turks, the Colonial Age would begin, Polish troops would march on Moscow and Martin Luther would question the immutable truths of the Catholic Church.

The first to move to Möhra was Luther's oldest recorded ancestor, Knight [Wigand von Lüder(n)](https://gw.geneanet.org/wolfalt?lang=en&n=luder&oc=0&p=wigand&type=tree), born around 1260. Around 1302 he settled on an estate between Möhra and Kupfersuhl in County Henneberg. Before the move he had a property on a tributary called Lüder of the river Fulda to the north-west of the town of Fulda. The town of Fulda used to separate Western and Eastern Franconia (*Francia occidentalis* vs. *Francia orientalis*). In 1330 Wigand von Lüder became a vassal of the princely (*Gefürstete*) [County Henneberg](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafschaft_Henneberg), located in the south-western part of the region of Thüringen as well as in northern Bavaria ("eine fränkische Grafschaft zwischen Thüringer Wald und Main und umfasste Gebiete der Rhön, des Grabfeldes und der Haßberge", to quote the Wikipedia).

Both Thüringen and Hesse were populated and governed by Franks. [Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#Die_Teilgruppen_der_Franken) states:

> Die Rheinfranken breiteten sich im Zuge der [Fränkischen Landnahme](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A4nkische_Landnahme) von [Köln](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ln) über [Mainz](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) bis ins heutige [Hesse](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessen)

and names the tribes of Chatti and Thuringi as close associates of the Franks:

> [Chatten](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatten): am Oberlauf von [Eder](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eder), [Fulda](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulda) und [Lahn](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahn) siedelnd (Namensgeber der späteren [Hessen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessen)).[[20]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-20) Sie waren ein eigenständiger Stamm, der im Zuge der Fränkischen Expansion unter fränkische Oberhoheit kam und sich mit den nach Südosten vordringenden fränkischen Siedlern vermischte.
>
> [Thüringer](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%BCringer) (und versprengte Kleingruppen anderer germanischer Stämme): sie waren vereinzelt an den und über den Rhein vorgedrungen und hatten sich dort niedergelassen. Erwähnt wird auch ein linksrheinisches „Kleinkönigtum“ der (im Jahr 531 in der [Schlacht an der Unstrut](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlacht_an_der_Unstrut) von den Franken geschlagenen) Thüringer,[[21]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-21) wenngleich dies in neuerer Zeit wieder umstritten ist.[[22]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-22) Diese Siedler gingen – im Gegensatz zu deren östlich verbliebenen Stammvölkern – in den Franken auf.

### Autochtonous Frankonian Origin Hypothesis

The *Populus hessiorum* lived, as we know from a 738 CE letter of Pope Gregory III to Bonifatius, along the lower stretch of the river Fulda in the 8<sup>th</sup> century. The Hessians likely descended from the [Chatten](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatten) that had been assigned to that specific area by the Romans. Since the beginning of the 6<sup>th</sup> century the Hessians were governed by the Franks but were given a certain degree of autonomy and thus were able to preserve their distinctiveness in laws and customs:

> Am Beginn des 6. Jahrhunderts gerieten die Chatten wohl unter die Oberhoheit der ausgreifenden Franken und wurden in das fränkische Königreich eingegliedert. Das Gebiet der Chatten diente den Franken anschließend als Ausgangsbasis für Feldzüge gegen die nördlich siedelnden Sachsen, die immer wieder in chattisches und fränkisches Gebiet eindrangen. Die Behauptung einer gewissen Teilautonomie der Chatten gegenüber den Franken führte dazu, dass sich ihr Stammesname in abgewandelter Form bis heute halten konnte.

This relative independence would indicate that the group lived in small communities in their habitat ever since the tribal migrations in the Roman times, stayed close-knit and assertive and was not diluted by outsiders pouring in from other parts of the Empire, even though Franks and Saxons kept encroaching on their land in the early Middle Ages – the former from the North-East, the latter from the North.

A member of the Hessian military class, the Knight of Lüder seems to have been valued, employed and rewarded by the Frankish rulers of Hesse and Henneberg. He ought to have been of a local, long-settled Franconian / Hessian descent, defined in early medieval samples by a mixture of Western Frankish and Southern European Y-DNA patterns.

However, the haplogroup that the Knight of Lüder passed along – downstream from I2a1b-L621 > CTS10228 > S20602 / Y3120 – appears to be specific to an overwhelming extent to Eastern Europe. Its presence in the Knight of Lüder from the River Fulda in a Frankish territory set quite deeply in the West and far away from the North Sea breaks out of the mold and would point to a origin that cannot be traced to the local Hessians and originated in a migratiory event.

Did his ancestrors arrived to Franconia from elsewhere?

### Migration Event Hypothesis: Saxon conquests

The land of the Hessians was south of the historic Saxony that extended out from the coast of the North Sea into the inner Germanic lands. Saxons conquered its northern part of Hesse and multiplied their efforts to force themselves even further to the South. Many raids against the Saxons, undertaken by the Franks, departed from Hesse.

However, it would seem rather unlikely that any number of Saxons could peacefully settle and become integrated in Frankish lands. Saxons were looking to conquer the locals, not to integrate among them.

### Migration Event Hypothesis: Slavic expansion to the Balkans

An 2019 essay on Luka Poropat's [South Slavic DNA Blog](https://southslavsdna.blogspot.com/2019/04/) rumbles through various Slavic expansion scenarios in the 6th and 7th centuries, and notes, quoting other academic sources:

> The second haplogroup, also known as I2a-Dinaric, is mainly represented by I-CTS10228 > I-Y3120 &hellip; subclades which had a very rapid formation and expansion since 1900-1700 YBP, "concordant with the timing of the Slavic ethnogenesis, considering that it takes a few centuries before one man can have enough male descendants to start having an impact at the scale of a population", and that "the higher percentage of I2a-Din in the south is probably just due to another founder effect due to the fact that the South Slavs originated in western Ukraine, where the ratio of I2a to R1a was higher".

At the same time L. Poropat's essay quotes [A genetic probe into the ancient and medieval history of Southern Europe and West Asia](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abq0755) by Lazaridis et al. 2022, that points out that Slavic migrations "profoundly affected the region" without, however, replacing the preexisting popuations.

> The reduction of Anatolian Neolithic ancestry was a long-term process in Southeastern Europe (1), which allows us to differentiate present-day populations from those preceding the Slavic migrations. When we order individuals along this component of ancestry (Fig. 5), we observe that present-day Slavs outside the Balkans have the least, whereas pre-Slavic inhabitants from the Balkans have the most of this type of ancestry,&hellip;

incidentally noting that medieval migrations were significantly less spontaneous that in the centuries prior when the population density was lower and societal organisation, much simpler:

> The medieval movements were carried out by large, organized communities engaging with complex states, such as the Avar Khaganate and Byzantine Empire, and no comparable polities existed in Yamnaya times.
>
> Considering the number of over 150 Bronze & Iron Age ancient DNA male samples from many Balkan countries from these studies giving a frequency of I-L621+ samples around 0-1%, the pattern is obvious, it would be a very, very rare and atypical subclade for the Bronze & Iron Age time periods in the Balkans.

Considering Luther's forefathers were integrated in the local population in Hesse and Thuringia and not living in a separate Slavic community (such large settlements did not exist West of Gießen and Dresden). They likely did not live in the Balkans either when our respected Y-DNA branches parted ways in the Iron Age. Even Slavic migrations could not have brought it. To begin with, S20602 / Y3120 is likely not even Slavic:

> Balto-Slavic speakers bear membership almost exclusively from two ancestral components&hellip; *k3* is omnipresent throughout European populations and decreases from north-eastern Europeans southwards. Thus, *k3* peaks in Baltic speakers and prevails in East Slavs (80–95%) and decreases notably in South Slavs (55–70%). In contrast, *k2* is abundant around the Mediterranean and in the Caucasus region and decreases among Europeans when moving northward.
>
> The presence of two distinct genetic substrata in the genomes of East-West and South Slavs would imply cultural assimilation of indigenous populations by bearers of Slavic languages

[Vajda’s Blog](http://blog.vayda.pl/en/i2a-dinaric-subclade-y3120-2/) states:

> It seems that the homeland of the haplogroup Din (Y3120) are the historical Black Sea steppes from Romania through Ukraine to the Volga. From these areas, genetic migration was to the west, to Poland, Slovakia and the Czech Republic. The second migration route would be from Romania to the Hungarian Plain. The third is from the north to Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Slovenia. All these Y-DNA migrations were no more than 2,500 years ago.

I assume ‘no more’ mean ‘no earlier’, i.e. in the historical Eastern European past, starting right in the middle of the Iron Age.

The question remains which route brought a Haplogroup Y3120 subclade from the Black Sea steppes to Frankish Hesse.

### Hungarian Connection

The mention of a "second migration route" to the Hungarian Plain seems to be pointing in the right direction.

FamilyTreeDNA's [Luther Surname DNA Project – Y-DNA Results Overview](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Luther?iframe=ydna-results-overview) page lists among the I-FT80992 matches at the Big Y-700 resolution under the heading "Eastern/Central European I-P37 line" next to a "Wiegand Von Luder b 1260" from Germany (1 match over 17,546 kits) a certain "Szurcsik Martin" from Hungary, whose STR markers differ somewhat from those of Wiegand Von Luder. (1 match over 1,931 kits) (The surname does have a Slavic ring to it despite its Hungarian spelling.)

History teaches that nomadic Magyar warriors after arriving in the Pannonian Plain in 896 and subduing the local communities, went on to conduct plundering raids that cut through Thuringia, Bavaria and Svabia and reached Eastern Franconia. The local rulers furiously pushed back. King Henry I repelled the Hungarians at the Battle of Riade in 933. King Otto I put a definitive end to Hungarian excursions to Frankish lands at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955.

["War History"'s page dedicated to the Battle of Lechfeld](https://warhistory.org/article/battle-of-lechfeld-and-the-magyar-threat) describes the presence of Hungarians at the gates of East Franconia at great length:

> In 894 nomadic Magyars raided into the Kingdom of Moravia, north of the Danube. <&hellip;> Under their leader Arpad, the Magyars entered Hungary to stay in 896, the year generally given for the founding of the Hungarian state. They easily subdued the scattered population of the central plain and then crushed Moravia in 906 and German forces in 907. A century later they conquered Transylvania.
>
> A long period of warfare followed. The Magyars ravaged Swabia, Bavaria, and Thuringia, obliging German princes to buy them off or incorporate them into their armies. After being defeated by King Henry I in 933, the Magyars shifted their attention elsewhere. In 934 and 942 they raided the Byzantine Empire, reaching Constantinople.

The defeat at Lechfeld seems to have persuaded the Hungarians to return to Hungary and switch to a more settled existence.

It is not clear if the defeated marauding Hungarian horse riders all returned to the Pannonian Plain to settle down in their Kingdom. It is tempting to imagine that some of them, including Luther's ancestors, settled down in some areas adjacent to East Franconia. This could not be necessarily related to the Battle of Lechfeld.

Hungaran warriors did not limit themselves to serving their chiefs and were also offering their services to the highest bidder. One year before the Battle of Lechfeld HUngarian mercenaries became imbroiled into a rebellion against King Otto I .

> Early in the year 954 the Magyars became involved in these civil wars as mercenaries. <&hellip;> The Liutpoldings&hellip; who had a tradition of alliances with the Hungarians had on the other hand the strongest motives to employ the Magyars to support the rebellion.

Opting for a larger military force happened to become catastrophic for the Hungarians:

> &hellip;this Hungarian expedition [of 954] reveals the basic weaknesses of forces that depend on steppe tactics alone. This incursion, which swept rapidly across a large part of Europe, was obviously made up solely of mounted archers. Although they wreaked havoc, moving about swiftly, surprising their victims, taking captives and devastating a few unprotected villages, they were unable to storm even poorly fortified localities. Incursions by mounted archers deep into the bowels of Europe with pillaging in mind were not very profitable. Steppe warriors were limited by the carrying capacities of their mounts, and captives could (and obviously did) slip away.
>
> The disappointing yield of the 954 expedition brought about a major change in Hungarian strategy. In 955 they invaded the East Frankish kingdom once again, but on this occasion with a very large army<&hellip;> Rather than moving rapidly as they had done the year before, dashing through Bavaria and Swabia into Rhine-Franconia, thence onto Lower Lothringia, Burgundy, and Italy before returning to their camps in the Carpathian Basin, this army moved at a glacial pace.

This led to the rise of the Holy Roman Empire on the one hand and to the establishment of the Kingdom of Hungary, on the other hand.

The battle at Lechfeld led to the rise of the Holy Roman Empire:

> The victory at Lechfeld brought Otto international recognition and led to the formal establishment of the Holy Roman Empire. Otto officially received the title of Holy Roman Emperor from Pope John XII in 962, assuming the mantle of Charlemagne as defender of the faith.

None the less, mounted Hungarian riders and archers kept offering their military services in the following centuries.

This presents us with an even more likely scenario: the Knight of Lüder could descend from a Magyar mercenary hired by some Frankish prince obtains citizenship rights in Franconia.

Ölbei Tamás' essay [Hungarian Mercenaries in the service of the Signoria Fiorentina (1360–1365)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378310330_HUNGARIAN_MERCENARIES_IN_THE_SERVICE_OF_THE_SIGNORIA_FIORENTINA_1360-1365) published in „Mercenaries and Crusaders”, © 2024, University of Debrecen „Hungary in Medieval Europe” and the HUN-REN–U, writing about much later phenomenon, mentions that

> Hungarians first served mainly in the German-led companies in the 1350s
> &hellip;
> The Italian cities employed German mercenaries along with a large number of Hungarian companies,&hellip;

Hence some coordination between Germans and Hungarians doubtless existed for some time, while full-out warring was rare. O. Tamás notes:

> It is true of medieval warfare in general, &hellip; that battles were the rarest form of warfare, and that raids, chevauchées and sieges were more typical.

How Hungarians were able to communicate with German and ENglish colleagues remains an open question. O. Tamás admits that, even though interpreters are mentioned in the historical documents, it is not fully understood

> In what form and language did the Hungarians, the English and the Germans communicate with each other. &hellip;communication was essential in armed contact. How the Hungarians fitted into this multilingual environment is almost unknown.

### Western Origin of Hg I2 Hypothesis

Yet there is a zeste of a possibility that Luther's I-FT80992 is a remnant of an old local population.

[BosnjackiDNK](https://bosnjackidnk.com/i2-staroevropski-istocni/) posits that in the early Holocene (a period between 11,650 and 7000 marked by a ca. 60m rise in sea levels) the Y-DNA haplogroup I2 was widely distributed in Europe from Spain to the southern reaches of England and Scandinavia over to the river Oder and all the way south down to Greece. Multiple expansions of R1b carriers out of Western Asia and the Pontic Steppe (to the Western boundaries of today's Poland and Romania) restricted I2 to the West of Europe. I2 may be linked to the Western Hunter-Gatherers. The source, however, quotes Lazaridis et al. 2016 that places I2-L596 among Anatolian farmers and Mathieson et al. 2015 that detects it in samples as far north as at Motala, Sweden.

> Njena uzvodna grana M423, koja se danas razgranjuje u dva osnovna ogranka (zapadnoevropski L161 i istočnoevropski L621) su utvrđene u drevnim kostima lovaca i sakupljača na prostoru Skandinavije, Njemačke, Švicarske, Iberije i Mađarske (Haak et al. 2015; Mathieson et al. 2015).

Thus it seems to be widely distributed in the original mixed population of Western Hunter-Gatherers (WHG) and Early European Farmers (EEF) throughout the Bronze and Iron Age until the Celtic migrations of the 4th-3rd centuries BCE.

[BosnjackiDNK](https://bosnjackidnk.com/i2-staroevropski-istocni/) refers to the megalithic burial sites at Beselich-Niedertiefenbach near Limburg an der Lahn, Germany where 2 male samples were tested positive for I2a-M423 (Immel et al. 2019) and posits that it was the Michelsberg culture (4400-3500 BCE) and its successor, the [Wartberg culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartberg_culture) (3600-2800 BCE) that developed I2a1b-L612 in the late Neolithic Period. Wikipedia chimes in:

> [Lipson et al. 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartberg_culture#CITEREFLipson_et_al.2017) examined the remains of 4 individuals buried c. 4000-3000 BC at the [Blätterhöhle](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bl%C3%A4tterh%C3%B6hle) site in modern-day Germany, during which the area was part of the Wartberg culture. The 3 samples of [Y-DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-DNA) extracted belonged to the paternal haplogroups [R1b1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b), [R1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1) and [I2a1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I-M438)

Blätterhöhle lies to the east of Hagen.

The same resource then concludes from a alleged high variance of I2a1b-L621 in the ancient samples in the area Baden-Württemberg that most downstream I2a1b SNP developed between there and *Western* Poland, i.e. in Germanic territory between Limburg and Thuringia.

It is not clear to me if this has anything to do with the third wave of migration from the *Yamna* culture who formed by mixture around 4000 BCE and expanded during the Early Bronze Age (3300 BCE). The Michelsberg culture seems to expand from France, not from Ukraine.

Medieval samples with I2a1b-CTS10228, in graves dating to a few centuries before Wigand von Lüder was born in Hesse, have been found in Eastern Europe. [DNAGenics](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1#when) lists many more I2a1 occurrences in ancient samples.

### Celtic Origin Hypothesis

The *Nature* article [Evidence for dynastic succession among early Celtic elites in Central Europe](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01888-7), discusses the Early Iron Age (800 to 450 BCE) in France, Germany and Switzerland, known as the ‘West-Hallstattkreis’, tracing the Early European Farmer (EEF) ancestry in the Celtic population of the South-Western Germany in the Late Antiquity andtraces the Early European Farmer (EEF) ancestry in the Celtic population of the South-Western Germany in the Late Antiquity and the early Middle Age.

> &#8230;the Celtic migrations during the fourth and third century BCE, not only ‘Celts’ migrated, but at least a limited number of people from northern central Europe reached the southern zone of the La Tène culture and even northern Italy
>
> The historical and archaeological record leave no doubt that the development of culture and population in southwestern Germany was temporarily characterized by profound discontinuities, particularly during the third to first century BCE. The definitive end of the 2,000 years of relative genetic continuity from the Bronze throughout the Iron Age in southern Germany is marked by a sudden, sharp increase of Steppe-related ancestry during the Late Antiquity and Early Middle Ages.
>
> While we cannot precisely date this migration, Roman and Late Iron Age data from Bavaria and Thuringia indicate that parts of the early Iron Age gene pool in southern Germany were not affected until the fourth or fifth century CE (with northern European ancestry not exceeding a median of 8% in these samples). In general, this turnover seems to be part of a larger movement of people, contributing northern European ancestry to the early medieval populations of England, Hungary, Italy and Spain.
>
> The northern regions of Germany (here Saxony-Anhalt, Lower Saxony, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Schleswig-Holstein) underwent a very different population genetic trajectory than southern Germany. While the Bronze and Iron Age populations in the north also received additional EEF ancestry, it was substantially less than what arrived in southern Germany, forming a Steppe ancestry-enriched gene pool highly similar to contemporaneous populations in Denmark, Sweden and Norway. Migration from northern Germany introduced EEF-depleted ancestry to southern Germany, resulting in a rise of the median northern European ancestry from 2.8% during the Iron Age to 62.5% during the Early Middle Ages, as well as in new paternal ancestry in the form of Y-chromosome haplogroups like I1-M253

### Scythian Origin Hypothesis

A. Lundiak in his [2023 blog](https://lundiak.wordpress.com/2023/01/07/ruriks-dna/#more-4997) links the I2a-Dinaric carriers to Scythian ancestry.

The Wikipedia article on the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) of the Pontic Steppe:

> Andreeva, et al. (2025) determined the paternal haplogroups of 36 Scythian males of the area stretching from the northern [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) coast to the [Middle Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_(river)), dated the 7th century BC to the 1st century AD. 58.4% of the Y-DNA haplogroups belonged to varieties of [haplogroup R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1a) (Y2631, Y934 and R-Y2) and [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1b) (R-Z2106). On the other hand, 22.2% belonged to [haplogroup I2a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I2a) (I-L801 and I-L702).

### Nordic Origin Hypothesis

Beside I1-M253, Scandinavia had ample R1a and R1b.

The 2021 article in the European Journal of Human Genetics 29 ([Subdividing Y-chromosome haplogroup R1a1 reveals Norse Viking dispersal lineages in Britain](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-020-00747-z)):

> Y-chromosome haplogroup R1a1 has been considered as a possible marker for Viking migrations because of its high frequency in peninsular Scandinavia (Norway and Sweden).

[First Scandinavians came from north and south](https://www.sciencenordic.com/archaeology-forskningno-society--culture/first-scandinavians-came-from-north-and-south/1453083) writes:

> Archaeologist and Stone Age expert, Mikkel Sørensen from the University of Copenhagen, Denmark, is more positive. In his [2013 study](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00293652.2013.770416), &#8230; concluded that Scandinavia was probably populated from both the south and the northeast, as the new study confirms.

[Northern European population history revealed by ancient human genomes](https://www.heritagedaily.com/2018/01/northern-european-population-history-revealed-ancient-human-genomes/118272) distinguishes between Western Hunter-Gatherers and Eastern Hunter-Gatherers, even though these two ancestries are presently mixed in Scandinavian populations:

> Previous analysis of ancient human genomes has revealed that two genetically differentiated groups of hunter-gatherers lived in Europe during the Mesolithic: the so-called Western Hunter-Gatherers excavated in locations from Iberia to Hungary, and the so-called Eastern Hunter-Gatherers excavated in Karelia in north-western Russia. Surprisingly, the results of the current study show that Mesolithic hunter-gatherers from Lithuania appear very similar to their Western neighbors, despite their geographic proximity to Russia. The ancestry of contemporary Scandinavian hunter-gatherers, on the other hand, was comprised from both Western and Eastern Hunter-Gatherers.
>
> “Eastern Hunter-Gatherers were not present on the eastern Baltic coast, but a genetic component from them is present in Scandinavia. This suggests that the people carrying this genetic component took a northern route through Fennoscandia into the southern part of the Scandinavian peninsula. There they genetically mixed with Western Hunter-Gatherers who came from the South, and together they formed the Scandinavian Hunter-Gatherers,” explains Johannes Krause, Director of the Department of Archaeogenetics at the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Human History, and senior author of the study.

### Extramarital Admixture Hypothesis

Or one of Martin Luther's ancestors in the paternal line did not descend from Wigand von Lüder but from a man in County Henneberg or County Mansfeld, whose Y-DNA would have connected him to the Slavic expansion from the South-East of today’s Poland and Polissya eastward in the 6th-8th centuries CE. The medieval Südharz was, however, not known for its Slavic communities. Even if some Slavs had come to the area, it is not clear by which process they could have integrated in a German tribal context. At least equally unlikely appears a wife’s infidelity to the point of bearing a male child in a well-to-do patriarchal family living in the countryside.

Or some Germanic tribes, such as the Goths, might have originated in the Pontic Steppe. Wikipedia mentions the Goths could just as well have migrated from the Black Sea area as expanded to it:

> Zur Zeitenwende siedelte im Bereich der Weichselmündung ein Volk, das antiken Autoren wie Tacitus unter dem Namen Gotonen (Gutonen; gotisch Gutans) bekannt war.
>
> Mit dem Ausgangspunkt, dass die Gutonen die Vorfahren der Goten waren, wird die Annahme gestützt, dass in der zweiten Hälfte des 2. Jahrhunderts ein Teil des Volkes nach Südosten zum Schwarzen Meer zog. Andere Forscher vertreten hingegen die Ansicht, dass die Goten erst im Schwarzmeerraum und damit im Vorfeld der römischen Grenze als eigene Völkerschaft entstanden seien (siehe Ethnogenese).

Probably during one or the other Migration Period (Celtic displacements of 4th-3rd century BCE, Roman Empire wars and the ensuing treaties, *Volkswanderung* of the 6-7th centuries CE), some carriers of Haplogroup I2a1 moved east and multiplied in Slavic communities, giving rise to many new SNPs, some stayed behind in the West and became a minority haplogroup carriers numerically overwhelmed by Celtic and Germanic new arrivals.

It is also possible that some Eastern Hunter-Gatherers from the Pontic Steppe wandered into Scandinavia from the East and eventually moved south to Hessia at the time of Germanic expansion.

According to the Diagenics Website the Y-DNA haplogroup [I2A1](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1#when) arose about 16,000 YBP and [I2A1B-L160](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1b#when) about 14,000 YBP both in Southeastern Europe. This is millenia before the Neolitic revolution and leaves more than ample time to settle anywhere in Europe.
