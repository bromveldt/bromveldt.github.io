The fun fact is that according to FamilyTreeDNA I (may) share a common paternal line ancestor with Martin Luther (1483-1546 CE). That ancestor lived around 450 BCE, relative recently, compared to the 20+ thousand years estimate I get on other famous DNA.

Even that is not certain.

The latest defining mutation Luther and I have in common is at level S20602 coincident with I-Y3120.
The latter is a distinctively East European haplogroup.

Luther inherited the mutation S20602 → FT80992 that had occurred before 1 BCE.

I inherited my haplogroup's defining mutation Y3120 → A16681 that had occurred around 100 CE.

How it is possible that the divergence took place at the dawn of the common era, I am not to know.
The Wikipedia puts I-CTS10228 at 5,100-3,400 YBP and I-Y3120 at 3,400-2,100 YBP, while [Genetic Homeland](https://www.genetichomeland.com/dna-marker/chromosome-Y/S20602) estimates S20602, believed to coincide with Y3120 / FGC12085 at 6,202 YBP. Can those estimates be down to an outdated state of research or to some discrepancies between the ISOGG, YFull and FTDNA phylogenetic trees?

My immediate matches on [YFull.com](http://yfull.com/) are from Poland (Lublin and Malopolska both at the eastern edge), one older DNA match in Sunghir () near Vladimir, Russia.

Let's trust FTDNA and assume that the MRCA lived around 450 BCE. That is the midpoint of Iron Age (the early Iron Age lasted from 800 to 450 BCE, the late Iron Age) in France, Germany and Switzerland, known as the ‘West-Hallstattkreis’. What happened then and more interestingly where?

Martin Luther was born in 1483 in Eisleben, County Mansfeld in the Holy Roman Empire (Grafschaft Mansfeld, Sachsen, Heiliges Römisches Reich) but before his birth his parents lived for a number of years in Möhra in County Thüringen, south-west from Mansfeld. His father's family called Möhra home for generations and was trying to get rid of its hereditary nobility - and quit serving in the military - for a more rewarding entrepreneurial pursuit (Martin Luther's father was a leaseholder of copper mines and smelters in the mining area Mansfeld-Südharz). The first to move to Möhra was Luther's oldest known ancestor, Knight [Wigand von Lüder(n),](https://gw.geneanet.org/wolfalt?lang=en&n=luder&oc=0&p=wigand&type=tree) born around 1260. Around 1302 he settled on a farm between Möhra and Kupfersuhl in Country Henneberg. Before the move he had a property on a tributary called Lüder of the river Fulda to the north-west of the town of Fulda. The town of Fulda used to separate Western and Eastern Franconia (*Francia occidentalis* and *Francia orientalis*). In 1330 he became a vassal of the princely [County Henneberg](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafschaft_Henneberg) (Gefürstete Grafschaft) , located in the south-western part of the region of Thüringen as well as in northern Bavaria ("eine fränkische Grafschaft zwischen Thüringer Wald und Main und umfasste Gebiete der Rhön, des Grabfeldes und der Haßberge", to quote the Wikipedia).

Incidentally and unsurprisingly, both Thüringen and Hessia were governed by the Franks. [Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#Die_Teilgruppen_der_Franken) states

> Die Rheinfranken breiteten sich im Zuge der [Fränkischen Landnahme](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A4nkische_Landnahme) von [Köln](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ln) über [Mainz](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) bis ins heutige [Hessen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessen)

and lists Chatti and Thuringi as close associates of the Franks

> [Chatten](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatten): am Oberlauf von [Eder](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eder), [Fulda](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulda) und [Lahn](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahn) siedelnd (Namensgeber der späteren [Hessen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessen)).[[20]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-20) Sie waren ein eigenständiger Stamm, der im Zuge der Fränkischen Expansion unter fränkische Oberhoheit kam und sich mit den nach Südosten vordringenden fränkischen Siedlern vermischte.
>
> [Thüringer](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%BCringer) (und versprengte Kleingruppen anderer germanischer Stämme): sie waren vereinzelt an den und über den Rhein vorgedrungen und hatten sich dort niedergelassen. Erwähnt wird auch ein linksrheinisches „Kleinkönigtum“ der (im Jahr 531 in der [Schlacht an der Unstrut](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlacht_an_der_Unstrut) von den Franken geschlagenen) Thüringer,[[21]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-21) wenngleich dies in neuerer Zeit wieder umstritten ist.[[22]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franken_(Volk)#cite_note-22) Diese Siedler gingen – im Gegensatz zu deren östlich verbliebenen Stammvölkern – in den Franken auf.

A member of the Hessian military class, the Knight of Lüder seems to have been acknowledged and rewarded by the Frankish rulers of Hessia and Henneberg. I would assume that he was of a local, Franconian / Hessian origin: *populus hessiorum* lived, as we know from a 738 CE letter of Pope Gregory III to Bonifatius, along the lower stretch of the river Fulda in the 8th century. The Hessians likely descended from the [*Chatten*](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatten) that were assigned to that specific area by the Romans. Since the beginning of the 6th century the Hessians were governed by the Franks but preserved their distinctiveness in their laws and customs.

> Am Beginn des 6. Jahrhunderts gerieten die Chatten wohl unter die Oberhoheit der ausgreifenden Franken und wurden in das fränkische Königreich eingegliedert. Das Gebiet der Chatten diente den Franken anschließend als Ausgangsbasis für Feldzüge gegen die nördlich siedelnden Sachsen, die immer wieder in chattisches und fränkisches Gebiet eindrangen. Die Behauptung einer gewissen Teilautonomie der Chatten gegenüber den Franken führte dazu, dass sich ihr Stammesname in abgewandelter Form bis heute halten konnte.

The land of the Hessians was next to the historic Saxony, its northern part was even conquered by Saxons, who multiplied their efforts to force themselves even further to the South. Many raids against the Saxons, undertaken by the Franks, departed from Hessia. It seems unlikely that any Saxons were integrated in Frankish lands for fear of helping out the enemy.

It could be expected that the Hessians stayed in their habitat ever since the tribal migrations in the Roman times, while Franks and Saxons encroached on their land in the early Middle Ages  – the former from the North-East, the latter from the North.

However, the haplogroup that the Knight of Lüder transmitted – downstream from I2a1b-L621 > CTS10228 > S20602 / Y3120 – appears to be surprisingly Eastern European for an territory situated quite deeply in the West. An atypical Y-DNA admixture would call for an attempt to explain it.

[Vajda’s Blog](http://blog.vayda.pl/en/i2a-dinaric-subclade-y3120-2/) states:

> It seems that the homeland of the haplogroup Din (Y3120) are the historical Black Sea steppes from Romania through Ukraine to the Volga. From these areas, genetic migration was to the west, to Poland, Slovakia and the Czech Republic. The second migration route would be from Romania to the Hungarian Plain. The third is from the north to Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Slovenia. All these Y-DNA migrations were no more than 2,500 years ago.

Would ‘no more’ mean ‘no earlier’?

[BosnjackiDNK](https://bosnjackidnk.com/i2-staroevropski-istocni/) posits that the Y-DNA haplogroup I2 was widely distributed in the early Holocene from Spain to the southern reaches of England and Scandinavia over to the river Oder and all the way South down to Greece. Multiple expansions of R1b carriers out of Western Asia and the Pontic Steppe (to the Western boundaries of today's Poland and Romania) restricted I2 to the West of Europe. I2 may be linked to the Western Hunter-Gatherers. The source, however, quotes Lazaridis et al. 2016 that places I2-L596 among Anatolian farmers and Mathieson et al. 2015 that detects it in samples at Motala, Sweden.

> Njena uzvodna grana M423, koja se danas razgranjuje u dva osnovna ogranka (zapadnoevropski L161 i istočnoevropski L621) su utvrđene u drevnim kostima lovaca i sakupljača na prostoru Skandinavije, Njemačke, Švicarske, Iberije i Mađarske (Haak et al. 2015; Mathieson et al. 2015).

Thus it seems to be widely distributed in the original mixed population of Western Hunter-Gatherers (WHG) and Early European Farmers (EEF) throughout the Bronze and Iron Age until the Celtic migrations of the 4th-3rd centuries BCE.

[BosnjackiDNK](https://bosnjackidnk.com/i2-staroevropski-istocni/) refers to the megalithic burial sites at Beselich-Niedertiefenbach near Limburg an der Lahn, Germany where 2 male samples were tested positive for I2a-M423 (Immel et al. 2019) and asserts that it was the Michelsberg culture (4400-3500 BCE) and its successor, the [Wartberg culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartberg_culture) (3600-2800 BCE) that developed I2a1b-L612 in the late Neolithic Period. Wikipedia chimes in:

> [Lipson et al. 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wartberg_culture#CITEREFLipson_et_al.2017) examined the remains of 4 individuals buried c. 4000-3000 BC at the [Blätterhöhle](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bl%C3%A4tterh%C3%B6hle) site in modern-day Germany, during which the area was part of the Wartberg culture. The 3 samples of [Y-DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-DNA) extracted belonged to the paternal haplogroups [R1b1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b), [R1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1) and [I2a1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I-M438)

Blätterhöhle is to the east of Hagen.

[BosnjackiDNK](https://bosnjackidnk.com/i2-staroevropski-istocni/) then concludes from a alleged high variance of I2a1b-L621 in the ancient samples in the area Baden-Württemberg that most downstream I2a1b SNP developed between there and Western Poland.

It is not clear to me if this has anything to do with the third wave of migration from the Yamna who formed by mixture around 4000 BCE and expanded during the Early Bronze Age (3300 BCE). The Michelsberg culture seems to expand from France, not from Ukraine.

Medieval samples with I2a1b-CTS10228, in graves dating to a few centuries before Wigand von Lüder was born in Hessia, have been found in Eastern Europe:

- 1 ancient sample (I10430) from Kars, Turkey, dated 750 CE. Kars is close to the border with Armenia and Georgia.
- 1 ancient sample at Niemcza south of Wrocław, Lower Silesia in south-western Poland tested positive for I2a1b-CTS10228 > Y3120. Dated 9th century CE (Stolarek et al. 2018; Zenczak et al. 2017).
- 9 ancient samples found at Karos and at Magyarhomorog, northern Hungary belong to I2a1b-CTS10228, of which 5 or 6 do not have the SNP S17250). Dated the Conquest Period 895-950 CE (Neperáczki et al. 2019; Fóthi et al. 2019).
- 1 Viking sample (VK53) found at Kopparsvik in Gotland, Sweden tested positive for I2a1b-CTS10228. Autosamal DNA results indicate 65% eastern European origin, placing the geographic origin in Pomerania or the Baltic shore. 10. or 11th century CE. (Margaryan et al. 2019).
- 1 Viking sample (VK542, [Gleb Svyatoslavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleb_Svyatoslavich)) with I-CTS10228 >Y3120 subclade found in Chernihiv, Ukraine  with predominantly Slavic ancestry. 11th century.

[DNAGenics](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1#when) lists many more I2A1 occurrences in ancient samples.

In the Nature article <NNN> traces the Early European Farmer (EEF) ancestry in the Celtic population of the South-Western Germany in the Late Antiquity and

> &mdlr;the Celtic migrations during the fourth and third century BCE, not only ‘Celts’ migrated, but at least a limited number of people from northern central Europe reached the southern zone of the La Tène culture and even northern Italy
>
> The historical and archaeological record leave no doubt that the development of culture and population in southwestern Germany was temporarily characterized by profound discontinuities, particularly during the third to first century BCE. The definitive end of the 2,000 years of relative genetic continuity from the Bronze throughout the Iron Age in southern Germany is marked by a sudden, sharp increase of Steppe-related ancestry during the Late Antiquity and Early Middle Ages.
>
> While we cannot precisely date this migration, Roman and Late Iron Age data from Bavaria and Thuringia indicate that parts of the early Iron Age gene pool in southern Germany were not affected until the fourth or fifth century CE (with northern European ancestry not exceeding a median of 8% in these samples). In general, this turnover seems to be part of a larger movement of people, contributing northern European ancestry to the early medieval populations of England, Hungary, Italy and Spain.
>
> The northern regions of Germany (here Saxony-Anhalt, Lower Saxony, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Schleswig-Holstein) underwent a very different population genetic trajectory than southern Germany. While the Bronze and Iron Age populations in the north also received additional EEF ancestry, it was substantially less than what arrived in southern Germany, forming a Steppe ancestry-enriched gene pool highly similar to contemporaneous populations in Denmark, Sweden and Norway. Migration from northern Germany introduced EEF-depleted ancestry to southern Germany, resulting in a rise of the median northern European ancestry from 2.8% during the Iron Age to 62.5% during the Early Middle Ages, as well as in new paternal ancestry in the form of Y-chromosome haplogroups like I1-M253

The Wikipedia article on the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) of the Pontic Steppe:

> Andreeva, et al. (2025) determined the paternal haplogroups of 36 Scythian males of the area stretching from the northern [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) coast to the [Middle Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_(river)), dated the 7th century BC to the 1st century AD. 58.4% of the Y-DNA haplogroups belonged to varieties of [haplogroup R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1a) (Y2631, Y934 and R-Y2) and [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1b) (R-Z2106). On the other hand, 22.2% belonged to [haplogroup I2a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I2a) (I-L801 and I-L702).

Beside I1-M253, Scandinavia had ample R1a and R1b.

The 2021 article in the European Journal of Human Genetics 29 ([Subdividing Y-chromosome haplogroup R1a1 reveals Norse Viking dispersal lineages in Britain](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-020-00747-z)):

> Y-chromosome haplogroup R1a1 has been considered as a possible marker for Viking migrations because of its high frequency in peninsular Scandinavia (Norway and Sweden).

[First Scandinavians came from north and south](https://www.sciencenordic.com/archaeology-forskningno-society--culture/first-scandinavians-came-from-north-and-south/1453083) writes:

> Archaeologist and Stone Age expert, Mikkel Sørensen from the University of Copenhagen, Denmark, is more positive. In his [2013 study](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00293652.2013.770416), he concluded that Scandinavia was probably populated from both the south and the northeast, as the new study confirms.

[Northern European population history revealed by ancient human genomes](https://www.heritagedaily.com/2018/01/northern-european-population-history-revealed-ancient-human-genomes/118272) writes:

> Previous analysis of ancient human genomes has revealed that two genetically differentiated groups of hunter-gatherers lived in Europe during the Mesolithic: the so-called Western Hunter-Gatherers excavated in locations from Iberia to Hungary, and the so-called Eastern Hunter-Gatherers excavated in Karelia in north-western Russia. Surprisingly, the results of the current study show that Mesolithic hunter-gatherers from Lithuania appear very similar to their Western neighbors, despite their geographic proximity to Russia. The ancestry of contemporary Scandinavian hunter-gatherers, on the other hand, was comprised from both Western and Eastern Hunter-Gatherers.
>
> “Eastern Hunter-Gatherers were not present on the eastern Baltic coast, but a genetic component from them is present in Scandinavia. This suggests that the people carrying this genetic component took a northern route through Fennoscandia into the southern part of the Scandinavian peninsula. There they genetically mixed with Western Hunter-Gatherers who came from the South, and together they formed the Scandinavian Hunter-Gatherers,” explains Johannes Krause, Director of the Department of Archaeogenetics at the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Human History, and senior author of the study.

A few possibilities come to mind:

Either **S20602** does in reality not coincide with Y3120 and is not at all indicative of I2a-Dinaric.

Or one of Martin Luther's ancestors in the paternal line did not descend from Wigand von Lüder but from a man in County Henneberg or County Mansfeld, whose Y-DNA would have connected him to the Slavic expansion from the South-East of today’s Poland and Polissya eastward in the 6th-8th centuries CE. The medieval Südharz was, however, not known for its Slavic communities. Even if some Slavs came to the area, it is not clear by which process they could have integrated in a German tribal context. At least equally unlikely appears a wife’s infidelity to the point of bearing a male child in a well-to-do patriarchal family living in the countryside.

Or some Germanic tribes, such as the Goths, might have originated in the Pontic Steppe. Wikipedia mentions the Goths could just as well have migrated from the Black Sea area as expanded to it:

> Zur Zeitenwende siedelte im Bereich der Weichselmündung ein Volk, das antiken Autoren wie Tacitus unter dem Namen Gotonen (Gutonen; gotisch Gutans) bekannt war.
>
> Mit dem Ausgangspunkt, dass die Gutonen die Vorfahren der Goten waren, wird die Annahme gestützt, dass in der zweiten Hälfte des 2. Jahrhunderts ein Teil des Volkes nach Südosten zum Schwarzen Meer zog. Andere Forscher vertreten hingegen die Ansicht, dass die Goten erst im Schwarzmeerraum und damit im Vorfeld der römischen Grenze als eigene Völkerschaft entstanden seien (siehe Ethnogenese).

Probably during one or the other Migration Period (Celtic displacements of 4th-3rd century BCE, Roman Empire wars and the ensuing treaties, *Volkswanderung* of the 6-7th centuries CE), some carriers of Haplogroup I2a1 moved east and multiplied in Slavic communities, giving rise to many new SNPs, some stayed behind in the West and became a minority haplogroup carriers numerically overwhelmed by Celtic and Germanic new arrivals.

It is also possible that some Eastern Hunter-Gatherers from the Pontic Steppe wandered into Scandinavia from the East and eventually moved south to Hessia at the time of Germanic expansion.

According to the Diagenics Website the Y-DNA haplogroup [I2A1](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1#when) arose about 16,000 YBP and [I2A1B-L160](https://www.dnagenics.com/haplogroups/ydna/I2A1b#when) about 14,000 YBP both in Southeastern Europe. This is millenia before the Neolitic revolution and leaves more than ample time to settle anywhere in Europe.
